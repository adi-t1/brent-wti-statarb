5. CASE STUDY: THE 2022 BRENT-WTI DISLOCATION
==============================================================================
Raw WTI-Brent differential, Oct 2021 - Dec 2022:
   start (2021-10-01 area) : -3.40 $/bbl
   widest (most negative)  : -11.39 $/bbl on 2022-07-29
   year-end 2022           : -5.65 $/bbl

What happened economically:
  Russia's invasion of Ukraine in Feb 2022 repriced WATERBORNE crude.
  Brent is the seaborne benchmark and absorbed the sanctions risk
  premium directly; WTI is landlocked at Cushing and only reaches
  global buyers through Gulf Coast export capacity. The differential
  therefore widened far beyond the transport-cost band that normally
  anchors it. Crucially this was a change in the EQUILIBRIUM, not a
  deviation from it -- exactly the case where a mean-reversion rule
  is structurally wrong.

How the model handled it:
  Fold 23 (train 2019-04-23..2022-04-20, test 2022-05-20..2022-11-17):
    training EG p-value 0.0423 -> gate PASSED, fold traded
    training half-life  17.7 days, beta 1.000
    trades in fold 5, net P&L -7.12 $/bbl

  2022 calendar-year out-of-sample P&L: -1.73 $/bbl gross, -3.28 net
  Trades entered in 2022: 6, of which 3 profitable net

2022-06-01 2022-06-02  long_spread             2   -2.045    1.556      0.290    0.150    0.140
2022-07-01 2022-07-05  long_spread             2   -2.470    0.332     -0.068    0.150   -0.218
2022-07-25 2022-10-11  long_spread            56   -2.202   -0.476     -5.170    0.350   -5.520

  The honest lesson: the training window ending April 2022 still
  contained enough pre-war data to pass the cointegration gate, so
  the model traded INTO a structural break and lost money doing it.
  A p-value computed on a 3-year window cannot detect a regime
  change that happened two months before the window ended. This is
  the fundamental limitation of the approach, not a tuning problem.
  Note what DID work: by the following fold the gate had absorbed
  the new data and the strategy re-adapted rather than compounding
  the loss.
